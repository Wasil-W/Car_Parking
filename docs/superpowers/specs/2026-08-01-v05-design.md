# v0.5 — what the app thinks, and how to correct it

Written 2026-08-01, while v0.4 was out for real-world testing. This is a design
for Wasil to argue with, not a decision. The section marked **"Needs your
decision"** is genuinely his to make; everything else has a recommendation with
the reasoning attached.

---

## What v0.4 already answered

The backlog's v0.5 entry lists four things. Two of them shipped in v0.4, and the
v0.4 spec's open questions 1 and 4 were answered by building it:

| Backlog item | Status |
|---|---|
| Home zone on the map, radius draggable | **Shipped in v0.4.** Tap to place, slider for radius, tap the circle to rename or remove. |
| Free zones as circles, added and removed there | **Shipped in v0.4.** Same interaction, dashed ring. |
| Correcting the parked pin | **Not built.** GPS drift still has no manual override. |
| Tariff-area polygons as a selectable overlay | **Not built.** |

Two of the v0.4 spec's four open questions are therefore closed by what got
built rather than by a decision:

- **Q1, drawing a free zone — drag a radius, or drop a pin and use a slider?**
  Answered: drop a pin, then a slider. Precision beats gesture on a phone held
  in one hand in the street, which is the same reasoning that put the slider in
  Settings originally.
- **Q4, does home-zone editing live on the map or in Settings?** Answered: the
  map. Settings keeps two read-only summary rows (`Home zone` → its address,
  `Free zones` → a count) and both open the map tab. See the comment at
  `SettingsScreen.kt:331`.

So v0.5 is the two remaining items, and they are the same idea twice.

## The spine: the app has beliefs you cannot see

Both remaining items are about the same gap. The app continuously decides two
things, and shows you neither:

1. **Where your car is.** `lastParkLocation` comes from a GPS fix with up to
   25 m of accuracy (`ParkDecisionEngine.MAX_ACCURACY_M`). When it is wrong,
   "Walk to car" walks you to the wrong street and there is nothing you can do
   about it.
2. **What that place costs.** `ZoneResolver.resolve()` runs point-in-polygon
   against 29 Amsterdam tariff areas and returns `Home`, `ManualFree`, `Paid`
   or `FreeStreet`. That answer decides whether the permit gets claimed and
   whether your brother is blocked — and it is invisible.

The second one matters more than it looks. When the app claims the permit and
you did not expect it to, or fails to claim when you did, the reason is almost
always which polygon the point landed in. Today the only way to find out is to
read the source.

**v0.5 makes both beliefs visible, and makes the first one correctable.**

That framing also sets the boundary. This release does not add a new data
source, does not change the claim rules, and does not add navigation. It shows
what is already there and lets you fix the one input that is genuinely
error-prone.

## Part 1 — correcting the parked pin

### The interaction

Tap the car marker. A card appears at the bottom of the map — the same slot the
zone candidate card already uses — saying **"Move the car pin"** with Cancel.
Tap the true spot. A ghost car marker appears there, the card now shows the
distance from the detected position ("42 m from where it was detected") with
Cancel and Confirm. Confirm writes it.

**Tap-to-place, not drag.** Three reasons:

- The app already teaches this exact gesture for zones. One interaction learned,
  used in two places.
- `MapCanvas` rebuilds its whole overlay list on every `update` pass
  (`MapCanvas.kt:89`), deliberately, so that a stale factory-time closure cannot
  keep answering taps with the first composition's parameters. A dragged marker
  has to survive across those rebuilds, which means special-casing marker
  identity — real complexity for a gesture that is worse on a phone anyway.
- A dragged pin is under your thumb at the moment you most want to see it.

The car marker currently swallows its own taps
(`setOnMarkerClickListener { _, _ -> true }`, `MapCanvas.kt:125`) to suppress
osmdroid's off-centre info bubble. That listener becomes the entry point.

**Only offered when there is something to correct:** `stateStore.parked` is true
and `lastParkLocation` is non-null. A pin for a car you are not parked in is not
a correction, it is a guess.

### What confirming does

Four writes and one existing call, in order:

```
stateStore.lastParkLocation = corrected
val zone = zoneResolver.resolve(corrected)      // re-run, do not assume
stateStore.lastZoneCode  = (zone as? Paid)?.area?.code
stateStore.parkedOutside = zone is ZoneInfo.Paid
SharedSync.requestSync(context)
```

No new plumbing. `SyncStateWorker` already reads exactly these three fields off
`PrefsParkStateStore` and pushes them to the other phone
(`SharedSync.kt:123-131`), so the correction reaches Walid's phone through the
path that already exists.

### Re-resolving the zone is the point, not a side effect

A 40 m error is the difference between a paid street and the free zone around
the corner. That is precisely the case where correcting matters: it changes
whether you should be holding the permit at all. Moving the pin without
re-resolving would leave the app confidently wrong about the thing that actually
matters.

### When the zone flips, ask — never act

If the re-resolved zone changes `parkedOutside`, the confirm card says so and
offers one button:

- **free → paid:** "This spot is paid parking (T13B, €5.37/h). Claim the
  permit?" → the existing claim path.
- **paid → free:** "This spot is not paid parking. Hand the permit back?" → the
  existing give-back path.

Never automatically. Auto-switch is the default for *detection*, where the app
is the only party who noticed anything happened. A correction is different: you
are standing next to the car, you just told the app something it did not know,
and you are the best-informed party in the loop. Asking costs one tap and
removes the entire class of "it did something because I mis-tapped".

### The 300 m cap

A correction beyond 300 m is refused, with "That is too far away to be a
correction — the app will pick this up as a new parking spot if you park there."

The reason is asymmetric damage. A mis-tap that keeps `parkedOutside = true`
harms nothing: the guard on Walid's phone reads the flag, not the coordinates,
so a wrong-but-still-paid location changes no decision. A mis-tap that lands in
a free zone flips the flag to false, and Walid's phone then lets him claim
freely while your car is sitting on a paid street with no permit. That is a
fine. 300 m is well beyond any plausible GPS error (accuracy is capped at 25 m
before a fix is even accepted) and well short of "different neighbourhood".

## Part 2 — the tariff areas, made visible

### The data is already here

This is the part that turned out much smaller than the backlog implies.
`android/app/src/main/assets/amsterdam_tarieven.json` is bundled (631 KB),
parsed at app start by `TariffAreas.parse()` into `TariffArea(code, name,
tariffText, polygons)`, and held in memory by `PermitApp`
(`PermitApp.kt:60-61`). `ZoneResolver` already runs point-in-polygon against it.

Measured, not estimated:

| | |
|---|---|
| Areas | 29 |
| Rings (outer + holes) | 170 |
| Total vertices | 17,503 |
| Largest single ring | 1,844 points |

29 polygons is nothing for osmdroid. **No new data source, no network call, no
parsing work, no download.** The whole of Part 2 is drawing what is already in
memory and letting a tap ask it a question.

### What the areas actually contain

All 29 are base-rate paid areas. A sample:

| Code | Rate | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `T11V` | €8.05/h | Basistarief TC1 ma-zo 00-24 |
| `T13B` | €5.37/h | Basistarief TC3 ma-za 09-24 |
| `T16C` | €3.01/h | Basistarief TC6 ma-vr 09-19 |
| `T17_UB01` | €0.10 first 3 h, then €1.72/h | Tarief 7 start tarief 10c |
| `T18A` | €1.29/h | Tarief 8 ma-vr 10-20, za 10-17 |

Two things worth recording:

- **The `V` suffix means round-the-clock, not "vergunning".** Every `V` code
  (`T11V`, `T12V`, `T13V`) carries `ma-zo 00-24`, and no other code does. It is
  tempting to read `V` as a permit marker and conclude the permit-versus-paid
  distinction is already in this file. It is not. That distinction needs the
  `gebruiksdoel` field from the `parkeerzones` API — see
  `docs/v0.6-zone-registry.md`.
- **The time windows are display-only,** consistent with the standing decision
  that tariff data is informational. The permit costs nothing to claim and has
  no hour budget, so nothing in the app branches on "ma-za 09-24".

### Drawing

Two states, because blanketing Amsterdam in 29 filled polygons would bury the
zone circles that the map exists to show:

- **Default:** only the area containing the car is drawn, as a thin outline with
  no fill, labelled with its code. This is the state that answers "why did it
  claim here?" without changing what the map looks like.
- **Toggled on:** all 29, light fill and thin stroke, via a small **"Tariff
  areas"** chip on the map. The chip sits in the existing bottom control card,
  which already solved the problem of controls floating illegibly over map tiles
  (`MapScreen.kt:184-195` — map tiles stay light whatever the app theme does).

Colour: a fourth quiet category, alongside `ZoneHome` / `ZoneFree` /
`ZoneCandidate`. It must not read as identity (whose car) or state (is something
wrong), and it must survive on light tiles in both app themes — the same
constraint that made the zone colours mode-independent (`Color.kt:97-102`). A
grey-blue at low fill alpha, contrast-checked against MAPNIK tiles the same way
the zone colours were.

Build the tariff overlays **once and cache them**, rather than rebuilding them
each `update` pass like markers and zone circles. They never change. Selection
highlighting swaps the stroke on one polygon, which is the only thing that needs
to be rebuilt.

### Selecting one

Tap a tariff polygon → a card showing code, name, rate, and hours. Nothing else,
and no action — it is a label, not a control.

This closes the v4 spec's **Q2** ("tapping is most natural, but a mis-tap shows
a wrong rate") by scope change rather than by choosing. When Q2 was written,
tariff comparison was still in scope and a mis-tap would have moved the permit.
Comparison has since been dropped; the tariff is informational. The worst
outcome of a mis-tap is now that you read the wrong rate for a second and tap
again. Tapping wins.

**Typing a tariff code directly is dropped.** The backlog asks for it, but it
has no consumer: the resolver works from geometry, the rate is informational,
and there is no override anywhere that a hand-entered code would feed. Building
an input for a value nothing reads is the definition of the thing YAGNI is for.
If a real use appears — most likely a manual override when the polygons are
wrong — it comes back with that use attached.

## One hit-test, one precedence

The map tap handler is about to serve three purposes: place a candidate, hit a
zone circle, hit a tariff polygon. That needs one rule, in one tested place.

`zoneHitAt()` in `MapZones.kt` becomes `mapHitAt()`, returning a sealed
`MapHit`, with this precedence:

The car marker stays outside this ladder. osmdroid does marker hit-testing
itself, and the car marker already has its own click listener — that listener
is what opens move mode (Part 1), and it consumes the tap before the map events
overlay ever sees it. `mapHitAt` decides only what a tap on bare map means:

1. **Placing mode wins outright.** While a zone candidate is being placed, or
   the car pin is being moved, every tap is a placement. Nothing else is tested.
2. **Zone circles** — home and free — using the existing nearest-centre rule, so
   a small zone inside a big one still wins (`MapZones.kt:38-46`).
3. **Tariff polygons**, only when the overlay is toggled on.
4. **Nothing.**

Zone circles are drawn as osmdroid `Polygon`s but deliberately return `false`
from their own click listener (`MapCanvas.kt:164`) so the tap falls through to
this one tested function rather than to osmdroid's geometry. The tariff
polygons follow the same rule, for the same reason.

Zones beat tariff areas because a zone is something Wasil placed by hand and a
tariff area is a neighbourhood-sized region that will almost always be under
the tap as well. The specific thing beats the ambient thing.

Keeping this a pure function over plain data — no osmdroid types, no Compose —
is what makes it testable on the JVM, which is how the existing zone hit-testing
is written and why it has tests at all.

## Testing

The house lesson from the last five releases stands: **every significant defect
was caught by looking at the screen, not by tests or review.** 185 passing tests
were blind to black-on-black text, an identity colour leaking app-wide, and two
invisible controls.

So, in both directions:

**JVM tests** (pure functions, no emulator):

- `mapHitAt` precedence, one test per rung of the ladder, plus the
  small-zone-inside-big-zone case that already has a test.
- Correction distance cap: accepted at 299 m, refused at 301 m.
- Re-resolution after correction: a point moved from inside a free zone to
  inside `T13B` flips `parkedOutside` to true and sets `lastZoneCode`; the
  reverse flips it back and clears the code.
- The zone flip **raises a prompt and does not claim** — assert the claim path
  is never invoked.
- `TariffAreas.parse` on the real bundled asset yields 29 areas with non-empty
  polygons. Cheap regression against a bad asset swap.

**On the emulator, by eye,** before anything is tagged:

- The tariff overlay in both app themes, since map tiles stay light in both.
- The overlay toggled on with zone circles present — are the circles still
  legible against 29 filled polygons?
- The move-pin card at the bottom of the map, which shares the slot with the
  zone candidate card.
- The confirm card in its flip state, which is the one screen that appears only
  in an unusual sequence and is therefore the one most likely to ship broken.

## Decided

Wasil answered all three on 2026-08-01.

**1. Correcting the pin updates the shared state — yes.** It writes coordinates,
zone code and `parkedOutside` through to Walid's phone via the existing
`SharedSync.requestSync()` path. It never claims or releases on its own, and it
refuses corrections beyond 300 m.

The reasoning that carried it: a human pointing at their own car is strictly
better data than a 25 m GPS fix, and the shared state exists so the other phone
knows the truth. Leaving a known-wrong location in place to avoid a hypothetical
mis-tap keeps the bad data on purpose.

**2. The 300 m cap stands.** It is a fat-finger guard, not a GPS one. The damage
case is narrow and specific: a mis-tap that happens to land inside one of the
free zones flips `parkedOutside` to false, which tells Walid's phone he is free
to claim while the car is actually on a paid street with no permit. A tap 2 km
away is not a correction at all — it is a different parking spot, and detection
will pick that up on its own. 300 m is far beyond any accepted fix (accuracy
worse than 25 m is rejected before a fix is used) and far short of a different
neighbourhood.

**3. The tariff overlay is built in full** — toggle plus tap-to-inspect — rather
than shrunk to outlining only the car's own area.

It is a diagnostic rather than a daily-use feature, and that was said plainly
before the call was made: its value is explaining the app's behaviour when it
surprises you. Wasil's reason for keeping it whole is that he wants to use it
before judging it, and a feature shrunk in advance cannot be evaluated. The
shrink remains available afterwards — it is a default and a hidden control, not
an architectural change.

**Default state: off**, with only the car's own area outlined until the chip is
tapped. 29 filled polygons would bury the zone circles the map exists to show.
Flipping the default is one line if the overlay turns out to be something he
wants up permanently.

## Out of scope, deliberately

- **The zone registry rebuild** — nearest payment machine instead of circles,
  and the `gebruiksdoel` permit-versus-paid distinction. That is v0.6 and has
  its own document: `docs/v0.6-zone-registry.md`.
- **The home-screen widget.** Still waiting on the map work settling, which is
  what this release does.
- **In-app navigation.** The app hands off to Google Maps on purpose, to avoid a
  Maps SDK dependency, an API key and a billing account.
- **Anything that changes the claim rules.** v0.5 shows the existing decision
  and corrects one of its inputs. It does not touch how the decision is made.
