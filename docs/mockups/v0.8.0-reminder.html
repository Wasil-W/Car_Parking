A time, not a countdown. “In 30 minutes” is a number you have to add to a clock you cannot see while walking. 09:00 is the thing you actually plan around, and it stays true if the notification sits unread for ten minutes — a countdown does not.

No holder is named, and this line is the second draft. The first read “… · Walid’s car has the permit”, which is a better sentence and one the app cannot honestly write: the only local record of who holds it is marked presentation-only precisely because it goes stale, and a stale name here would be a guess published as a fact about the one subject this project has a rule about. Claim permit is the existing ACTION_CLAIM, and it asks the live question instead — if Walid’s car really is parked out there, the guarded claim answers with the blocked notification rather than quietly stealing it. Pinned as a test so it cannot drift back in.

No buttons. There is nothing the app can do about this one, and it should not pretend there is.

Ten minutes, not thirty. The two leads differ on purpose. Being told late that you owe costs a fine; being told late that it is free costs a few minutes of meter. A thirty-minute lead here would be a notification you cannot use yet.

Green, not rust. FineLight #4C7645 against AlertLight #96412F — the state pair the app already owns. Neither is identity colour, so neither can be misread as “whose car”.

A channel each, so you can silence this one. They are not the same kind of message: one can save a fine, the other saves a few minutes of meter, and on a single channel you would have to mute the useful one to be rid of the chatty one. Before you start paying is HIGH and heads up; When parking turns free is DEFAULT and does not. Android’s own controls settle it, with no settings screen of our own.

Then looked at

What the shade changed

Both notifications were posted for real on an API 35 device before this page was finished, because in this project the screen has caught every defect the tests did not. Two things came back.

The long case clipped. A stepped rate, a double-barrelled neighbourhood and a street name rendered as from €1,72/h · Oostelijk Havengebied · Cr… — and expanding the card showed the same clipped line. The truncation order was at least right (the time is in the title, the rate comes first), but the street is what tells you which car this is about. Fixed with BigTextStyle, so expanding now shows the whole line. Nothing in 597 unit tests could have found that; it took one screenshot.

Everything else held. Title on one line, actions where they should be, the quiet one correctly button-less.

The chain

One wake-up books the next

A parked car needs a sequence of alarms, not one. Each run of the worker asks what the spot is doing, says something if a boundary is inside its lead window, then books exactly one successor — the same shape the drive-time location sampler already uses. Both strips below are the city’s real bundled data, drawn to scale.

T16B Basistarief TC6 · ma–za 09–19 · €3,01/h — the ordinary case
T17N Basistarief TC7 · 19–06, niet za op zo · €1,72/h — the one that already caught a bug

Checked against all 29 areas, not against these two. A test parks a car at Monday 00:00 in every bundled area, follows the chain for a full week, and requires that every moment those areas start charging was preceded by a reminder inside its window. An off-by-one in the booking would show up there as a missed transition — which is a fine, in real life.

Restraint

Four conditions, and it stays silent unless all four hold

ConditionWhy it is load-bearing
parked Nothing is owed about a car being driven. Drive off and the open park record closes, so the next wake-up finds nothing and the chain stops on its own.
in a paid area Geometry, not the clock — a polygon hit. Home, a marked free zone and a free street owe nothing at any hour.
a boundary inside
the lead window		 Thirty minutes for owing, ten for free. Outside that there is nothing yet worth interrupting anyone for.
the permit is not
already settling it		 The one that is easy to miss. The app claims on geometry regardless of the hour, so a permit holder parked overnight in a 09–19 zone already holds it and owes nothing at 09:00. Reminding them would be noise about a problem the app solved on arrival. This is the obligation/settlement split doing its job.

And a fifth, which is a silence rather than a condition. A park whose position never resolved gets nothing — D9. Not “you are fine”, and not a guessed area: a failed fix is a gap in what we know, and this project has a standing rule against publishing one as a fact. The way out is the pin the map already offers — place it and the chain schedules itself.

Not settled here

What this mockup does not decide

  1. Are 30 and 10 the right numbers? Thirty is roughly a walk back to a car in a city; ten is enough not to pay for the tail. Both are guesses that have never met a real morning. They are two constants and cost nothing to change.
  2. What about a park that starts while the meter is already running? Arrive at 11:00 with no permit and you are owing immediately — and this feature says nothing until 18:50, because there is no boundary ahead to warn about. That is arguably the park notification’s job rather than the reminder’s, but right now neither of them says it.
  3. What happens on a three-day park? The chain keeps going, so a car left outside from Friday to Monday collects six notifications about one spot. A per-park cap, or a rule that goes quiet after the first unacknowledged one, is not written yet.
  4. Does it survive Doze? Thirty minutes of lead is deliberate slack for exactly that, and a phone walking around town is unlikely to be dozing deeply — but this is the one claim on the page that only a real overnight park can settle, not a test.
